# GitTurtle design system

An original native desktop workspace for everyday Git work and reading history. Prioritize clear targets, legible relationships, effortless comparisons, and immediate input feedback. Midnight combines deep slate surfaces with a mint accent and remains the default; Braden is the original light palette. Graphite, Tokyo Night, Catppuccin Mocha, Nord, Solarized Dark, Solarized Light, One Dark, One Light, Rosé Pine, Rosé Pine Dawn, Dracula, Alucard, Kanagawa Wave, Kanagawa Lotus, Porcelain, Sandstone, Deep Sea, and Ember provide distinct alternatives. Braden retains the `daylight` storage identifier and its original palette. Porcelain and Sandstone are additional light palettes; Deep Sea and Ember are additional dark palettes, originally designed for GitTurtle. Repository content fills the window; keep decorative charts, gradients, and oversized empty headers out of the working views. Projects and Settings can use grouped surfaces to make their choices easy to find.

This is the canonical current design contract. [The active native polish milestone](docs/native-polish-milestone.md) owns acceptance status, screenshots, measurements and final build identities; design rules and implemented controls do not establish that a pending native check passed. [The former design page](docs/design.md) is a compatibility link, and dated evidence keeps its original names and build identities.

## Shared control contract

Use the existing native Button and shared app helpers for actions. At the default interface size, the compact app helper sets 28 px height/minimum width and 10 px horizontal padding for labeled actions; icon-only actions use an explicit 28 px square with 6 px horizontal padding so the artwork is not clipped by the inner content wrapper. Labeled controls use a 6 px icon/label gap; named Git toolbar actions use 34 px height, and the profile picker and commit action use 36 px. Shared corners are 7 px, with a 12 px large-surface token. Icon controls use the complete allocated target and a specific accessible name. Keep hit areas, borders and padding fixed across hover, pressed and focus states.

These explicit app dimensions coexist with the pinned toolkit's rem-based input, switch, menu and direct Button geometry. Native spacing and controls scale with the 13 px root rem, while explicit app dimensions use interface-size/13. The focused component patch preserves explicit app text sizes, icon sizes and icon/label gaps instead of replacing them inside the control with toolkit size-category defaults. Controls without an explicit override keep their toolkit defaults. Do not assume that `.small()` alone applies the 28 px app helper. Use a small spacing rhythm, balanced padding and full-row hit areas; native validation checks the actual resulting targets.

Resting secondary controls use panel/subtle surfaces; hover uses the palette hover surface, pressed uses selected, and keyboard focus uses a visible accent ring. The transparent compact helper paints both states as translucent layers that turn a panel into exactly hover or selected and repeat nearly the same step on any other surface, so an action inside a hovered or selected row lifts again instead of vanishing into the row (`Palette::control_fill`); its label keeps the text rule on every composited fill, moving toward black or white only in a palette whose `text` would fall below it (`Palette::control_label`). A ghost Button, the app's or one the toolkit draws for it, takes the same hover and pressed layers (`Theme::ghost_hover`, `Theme::ghost_active`), and a selected one keeps the pressed layer; the toolkit's own derived ghost fills barely move a dark surface. Secondary controls such as the branch and profile pickers, Fetch and Pull rest on `subtle`, so every palette, built-in or fitted, holds `hover` at least 1.08:1 off `subtle` as well as off the panel. Every focused Button, the helper in any variant or a Button built directly, draws a 2 px ring of full accent 1 px outside its edge, like the picker card's ring (`appearance::BUTTON_FOCUS_RING`, installed as the toolkit's `Theme::button_focus_ring` where the palette sets `ring`). The gap keeps the ring off a primary or danger fill, so ring and gap lie on the surface beneath, where the accent rule holds 3:1 on every readability surface in every state the helper paints, hovered and pressed on rows included; no such state fades the whole Button, which would fade the ring with it. The ring takes the toolkit's 3 px outside the edge and moves nothing, so a clipping parent or an adjacent segment cuts it where it cut the toolkit's ring. The repository tab strip's scrolling list, the Tags dialog body, the Reflog content, the Tags and Reflog lists, the branch chooser's list, the worktree manager's content, its list of worktrees and its list of branch choices when creating a worktree, the tag inspector's list of Push to… buttons, the project hub's list of recent projects, Quick Open's form and the Keyboard Shortcuts body keep the ring's width plus gap as room inside their clips and give it back through their margins (the dialogs also through the gap above the footer, and the worktree manager, Quick Open and Keyboard Shortcuts through their titles' margin; the hub's list through its panel's padding), so at whole scale factors nothing moves. The tab strip is the window's first row, so it is never shorter than a tab or close button plus that room above and below and its bottom rule: below about 0.875 of the default text scale it grows by the few pixels it lacks, and above that it keeps its height. Still open: macOS rendering and fractional scale factors, where the 1 px gap and 2 px ring need not land on whole device pixels. Selected controls keep a persistent surface and a checkmark or selected/expanded semantic state; hover remains distinguishable. While it is enabled, a hovered selected helper blends its `selected` fill toward accent as a hovered selected row does (`Palette::row_hover`), at least the pressed step (6 of 255 in one channel) from its resting fill, with its label at the text rule; a disabled one keeps its disabled look. Primary actions use their separate accent foreground and resting/hover/pressed tokens; disabled, a primary action (Push, Commit) rests on the neutral `subtle` fill a disabled secondary control takes, not a tint of the accent, as does a disabled selected helper (the command palette's Open command), so an unavailable Push reads like the unavailable Fetch and Pull beside it. Where that fill stands off the surface beneath it by less than 1.05:1, as the commit action's equals the composer's own `subtle`, Open command's equals the raised surface of most Omarchy dark palettes and the toolbar's Fetch, Pull and Push stand 1.046:1 off Midnight's toolbar, the disabled Button keeps its shape with a 1 px edge in the popup edge colour, 1.4:1 or more on every page surface (`Palette::disabled_edge`, through `appearance::keep_disabled_shape`); it moves nothing, a Button as wide as its content taking the edge's pixel from its padding. Disabled controls dim and suppress activation: a disabled Button, Switch label, menu item with its shortcut, or command palette row draws its label in one dim, secondary text at half strength or at the first step stronger, up to three quarters, that reads 3:1 on the page's surfaces and the menu's and on each disabled Button's fill, the kit's default Button's translucent one included (Settings' Save on its card, where a dim fitted without it read 3.002:1 in Midnight and below 3:1 in Nord and Solarized Dark), with 0.05 of room for 1x rendering wherever that step keeps the 60% bound below (all but Kanagawa Lotus) (`Palette::disabled_label`, installed as the toolkit's `Theme::disabled_label`), so it reads about 3:1 in light palettes, where half strength fell near 2:1, keeps at most 60% of enabled secondary text's contrast, and is never plain secondary text, which a theme may set as strong as text; a menu's label, the text that titles or explains its items such as the project pane's row name, the profile menu's heading or the review Options menu's caption, is not an item and is never drawn as an unavailable one: it takes the section-label style (see Typography and density), 11 px medium in plain secondary text, beside items at the menu's 11.4 px regular (both scaled with the interface text), so weight sets a heading apart where a palette's secondary text is nearly its text, as in One Dark and Solarized Dark, and a heading no larger than its items never outweighs the commands it titles (`appearance::SECTION_LABEL_TEXT`, installed as the toolkit's `Theme::menu_label_size`); it is announced as text, not as one more command, and Up and Down pass over it, as they pass over separators and disabled items, so the first Down in a menu that opens with a label reaches its first available item; a disabled Switch fades its thumb with its track to half strength; a checked Switch's thumb takes the accent's label colour, at 3:1 or more on its track in every palette; a focused enabled Switch draws the Button ring around its track, and hovering an enabled unchecked Switch, its label or the Settings row it fills included, moves its track by the palette's row hover step (`hover` less `panel`, `Palette::switch_hover`), at least 6 of 255 in one channel, while a checked track keeps its accent and a disabled Switch shows neither; do not use a pointer cursor or hover-only promise of an available action. Every icon action has a specific accessible name. The pinned Button and the kit's menu items include focused local patches exposing disabled state to AccessKit while preserving input blocking. Keep visible state, accessible names and nearby disabled reasons consistent. Current work includes VoiceOver, keyboard navigation and available system display settings; follow the [native accessibility contract](docs/native-accessibility.md) and record actual build-specific results.

Inventory shared controls by workflow: page navigation and profile picker; Git toolbar and Targets; branch/worktree folders and rows; history/column controls and commit inspector; changed/working file rows and staging; comparison modes/Find/image/document tools; project actions and recents; settings cards/switches/size controls; command palette rows; review forms and confirmation/cancel buttons. Native review must exercise the rendered hover/focus/disabled states, not only palette values.

## Navigation transition table

| Event | Destination and retained context |
| --- | --- |
| Select a commit | Remain in History; load only metadata/files. Explicit file activation enters Compare. |
| Enter an inspection | Retain the previous comparison, exact targets, selection, query, scroll and focus in the bounded existing inspection chain. |
| Back / primary-[ | Close the top inspection first, otherwise return from Compare/Changes to retained History. At root History, open Projects; page Back returns to its named preceding page. |
| Escape | Dismiss the active dialog, menu, Find or other transient surface first. Return through inspections or the current page. At root History, clear its query and restore History focus. |
| Explicit History | Exit repository inspections and show retained History. It is not a clear-query, reset-columns, or discard-drafts action. |
| Explicit Projects | Show only the project hub, with no repository status/errors/previews or hidden editor focus. Retain the current repository for a named return action. |
| Reopen the currently retained project | Restore its existing mode, inspection chain, selections, queries, scroll, pane sizes, focus and drafts. |
| Choose a different repository | Initialize History for the newly resolved canonical worktree. Clear previous repository targets/results; load only the new worktree's own persistent drafts and identity. Appearance and column preferences stay global. |
| Settings | Retain the preceding page and repository context; Back names and restores that page. Repeated visits and appearance changes preserve unfinished fields. Identity fields initialize for a different canonical repository or explicit Reset; unedited fields follow effective Git identity. |
| Cancel a modal | Cancel replaceable reads, refuse late reopening, restore attached visible focus. Accepted Git operations use their separate explicit Cancel and are never replayed. |

Keep one return action in the upper-left page navigation position. At root History it is the labeled Projects action; within comparisons and Settings it is a familiar Back arrow with the exact destination in its accessible name and shortcut tooltip, labelled “Back” where the repository header's full form gives it the labelled Projects' slot. Do not duplicate Projects beside the root return action. Comparison content can also provide its local Back beside the path; both invoke the same one-level return. Reset controls name their exact scope, such as Reset column layout or Clear search. Page navigation and repository shortcuts must not act beneath a modal or on a hidden Projects workspace. Restore focus only after its visible destination attaches; late previews may retain content but cannot navigate or construct hidden editors. The [navigation contract](crates/app/docs/navigation-and-refresh.md) records the retained-state boundaries.

## Semantic palette ownership

[`appearance.rs`](crates/app/src/appearance.rs) owns all twenty built-in palettes, shared by native controls, custom drawing, and editor surfaces. Midnight uses a `#10151F` canvas, `#171E2B` panels, and `#75E0BB` mint accent. Braden uses soft white surfaces with evergreen actions. Subtle surfaces group content without competing with the selected row; primary actions have distinct resting, hover, and pressed colors with their own readable foreground.

Follow system selects Braden in system Light appearance; in Dark appearance it uses the selected dark theme, or Midnight when the saved selection is light. It never overrides the Linux Omarchy theme, which follows the desktop instead. Theme application updates both native component color tokens and editor/drawing colors together. Appearance, density and independent text-size preferences remain global across profile changes.

The curated themes adapt the primary [Tokyo Night palette](https://github.com/tokyo-night/tokyo-night-vscode-theme), [Catppuccin Mocha palette](https://catppuccin.com/palette/), [Nord palette](https://www.nordtheme.com/), and five families chosen in the [theme palette research](docs/development/2026-09-17-theme-palettes.md): [Solarized](https://github.com/altercation/solarized/blob/62f656a02f93c5190a8753159e34b385588d5ff3/README.md) (Solarized Dark and Solarized Light); One, from Atom's [One Dark](https://github.com/atom/one-dark-syntax/blob/9c96f4454362267ac45322063e193ccf9d2debb1/styles/colors.less) and [One Light](https://github.com/atom/one-light-syntax/blob/d84579027410c576086dfca14d934c4bd74b0438/styles/colors.less) syntax themes; [Rosé Pine](https://github.com/rose-pine/palette/blob/92af52b465ab6e47437aca223c9b8d3009a2023b/palette.json) (Rosé Pine from its Main variant, and Rosé Pine Dawn); [Dracula](https://github.com/dracula/dracula-theme/blob/5962daae54e4608d281cb6f4eee2349e605d9e3c/README.md) (Dracula and its light variant Alucard); and [Kanagawa](https://github.com/rebelot/kanagawa.nvim/blob/bb85e4bfc8d89b0e62c8fa53ccdd13d12e2f77b3/lua/kanagawa/colors.lua) (Kanagawa Wave and Kanagawa Lotus; the Dragon variant is not shipped). These are native palette adaptations; the code editor's background, foreground, gutters, diff decorations and syntax colors all follow the chosen theme. Keywords and preprocessor directives take `renamed`, strings `added`, numbers and constants `warning`, types `modified`, functions `hunk`, tags, attributes, properties, links, labels and titles `accent`, comments and hints `muted`, and variables, operators and punctuation `text`; emphasis keeps only its italic or weight. A role that does not already read is mixed toward `text` until it reads at 4.75:1, or at the palette's own text contrast where that is lower but never below 4.5:1, on the canvas, the panel and the added and removed line tints; a custom theme whose text misses 4.5:1 there draws all syntax in `text` ([roles and fitting](docs/development/themes/spec.md#diff-and-syntax-colors)). The highlights behind code are fitted instead of the syntax: the editor selection, Find's matches and current match, and the changed-word tints are derived from `selected`, `accent` and the line tints so text and every syntax color keep at least 4.5:1 on them, and each stands at least 0.04 ΔEOK (OKLab distance) from the line it sits on. Rows and lists keep `selected`.

## Typography and density

The dimensions below describe the default 13 px interface and 12 px code sizes. Settings independently adjusts interface text from 11–18 and code text from 10–24, with separate reset controls. Scale relevant row/control heights and editor gutters with their text; retain selection, focus and viewports when changing sizes. Larger sizes must keep the minimum 1,000 × 680 content window usable through appropriate scrolling.

- UI: system sans serif, 13 px regular; 13 px medium for selected labels and panel headings. macOS uses the platform UI font; Linux embeds IBM Plex Sans (Regular, Medium, SemiBold and Bold, with italics) so every weight draws as its own face on every distribution, and multiplies both text sizes by the desktop text scaling factor on Wayland. Its antialiasing comes from the first source that answers: the settings portal's GNOME `font-rendering`, then `font-antialiasing`; then fontconfig's resolved `sans-serif` `antialias`/`rgba`, distribution defaults included; then grayscale; see the [Linux runbook](docs/linux.md#platform-limits).
- Code, hashes, and aligned numerical data: monospace, 12 px, with gutters and aligned overlays using the native editor’s measured line height. macOS uses Menlo; Linux embeds DejaVu Sans Mono so code keeps its columns on every distribution, whether or not the family is installed. The embedded font stays the default, except on a first launch on Omarchy, which turns the setting below on so code matches the desktop. Below **Code text size**, Linux Settings offers **Use the desktop's monospace font**. When it is on, code uses fontconfig's `monospace` family (JetBrainsMono Nerd Font on Omarchy), but only a family the toolkit loaded that draws basic Latin at one advance. Otherwise the row gives the reason and code keeps the embedded font. The family is looked up off the interface thread and again whenever a window regains focus. Code text shows the characters as typed: it turns off the `calt` and `liga` font features, so a ligature font such as JetBrains Mono draws `--`, `->` and `!=` as separate characters in diffs, editors, Blame, the conflict editor, Markdown code blocks, the PR review and the Settings sample. The PR review sizes its line-number columns from the code font's digit width at the current code size, so the file's widest line number fits. Code text must support selection and copying.
- Main repository title: 14 px semibold. Commit title in the right inspector: 15 px medium, fully wrapped, followed immediately by the available body at 12 px with a comfortable 1.5 line height. Avoid large display typography in repository views.
- Section labels: 11 px medium, the navigation's headings and a menu's labels alike (`appearance::SECTION_LABEL_TEXT`), restrained letter spacing, uppercase only for short headings such as LOCAL BRANCHES. No all-uppercase sentences.
- Spacing: use a restrained 4/8/12/16 px rhythm for explicit app dimensions; native rem-based spacing follows the interface size. Keep pane and card padding consistent within each surface. A dialog or panel, and each column within one, starts its text at one left edge, its header, rows, labels and notices alike: the GitHub panel's, and its review file column's, at a compact Button's 10 px label inset from that edge, where the Buttons at the edge start their labels, the Reflog's at its fields' 11 px; boxes and bordered surfaces keep the edge and their own padding. Reserve pill shapes for small reference labels and counts.

Navigation rows remain compact; a selected navigation or project-list row keeps its label in text at medium weight and marks its icon in the accent. Use full-row hit areas. Keep metadata baseline-aligned and columns consistent. Column titles and text/reference cells share a 10 px leading inset; the graph uses a matching 10 px node margin. Preserve this geometry when resizing or scrolling horizontally.

## One responsive order

Every page gives way to a narrower window, or a larger interface size, in one order, whatever control it is laying out:

1. **Content stays whole.** Repository content and what identifies it keep their room first: the commit subject (at least its floor), a file's name and its Compare path, the selected item, and the text being read or written. Code and images scroll in their own viewports; the chrome around them never cuts them for a control.
2. **Actions become icons.** A labelled action keeps its 28 px control with its icon, its whole label as its accessible name and its tooltip, as Blame, Directories, Stage, Unstage and the header's Projects and profile do.
3. **Labels go.** Captions, group names and words beside a count leave next; the glyph or count that tells the item apart stays, with the words in its tooltip and accessible name.
4. **Secondary columns go.** Columns after the content (History's Author, Date and SHA, a panel's second column) leave the visible width last, past the table's edge where they scroll sideways, or below the content where a panel stacks.

A control's own breakpoint follows from this order, measured from the interface font at the interface size, rather than a figure of its own; where a breakpoint described below disagrees with the order, the order holds. Whatever the width, nothing is cut by its clip: a line of text fits its room, wraps inside it, or ends in an ellipsis at a character boundary; a control lies wholly inside the region that clips it, a row that cannot hold its controls wraps them below whole; a column at a table's edge is whole or past the edge. No caption or count is drawn without the control it belongs to, and no separator ("·") stands alone. The width walk (`crates/app/src/width_walk.rs`) lays out History, Compare (text and image), Working Changes and the GitHub panel from 461 to 1,480 px at 13 and 18 pt in 490 and 980 px tall windows, at each measured breakpoint and a pixel either side of it, and fails on cut or orphaned text, on the Git action bar's repository edge left out or its line drawn short of its whole text, on a bar control running into the next, on the navigation's mode toggles wrapped onto a second row or its keyboard hint wrapped inside a phrase, and on a field's placeholder wider than its field (History is walked again on a detached HEAD, on a branch with no upstream, with the navigation at its 180 px minimum, History and Working Changes with the inspector saved at its widest, 480 px, which every narrower window draws narrower, giving way to the content column's minimum, with a search that found nothing, where it fails on the search's line or Clear search not wholly inside the list and the window, with a write's outcome fresh in the status line, where it fails on the outcome drawn short of its whole text, and beside the local watch's warning, where the outcome may end in an ellipsis only once every hint and Details…'s label have given way; History and Working Changes are walked again with that warning alone); a finding a follow-up owns is listed there by name until it is fixed. A dialog with a width of its own is never wider than the window less 24 px on either side (`appearance::dialog_width`; the custom theme editor keeps its own 32 px rule), and in a 461 × 490 half tile at 13 and 18 pt the walk opens every such dialog and fails one closer to a side than that, one past the window's foot, text that crosses its clip or runs into the panel's padding, and a list that no longer shows its first row whole.

## Motion

Motion explains a change the user caused; nothing else moves. Each of the app's own transitions lasts 120–180 ms on an ease-out curve, fast from its start and settling into its end, with no delay, spring, bounce or overshoot. It never holds back input, layout or a frame's content: the first frame after the change lays out the final state, and the transition only changes where one element is drawn on its way into that layout, ending exactly where the layout puts it. It draws only what is on screen and asks for frames only while it is drawn.

- **A row moving between Staged and Unstaged.** When a file's own Stage or Unstage, from its row's control or its menu, moves it to the other group, the row travels for 150 ms from where the user left it in the window to its new slot, drawn above the rows it passes, beneath the Staged and Unstaged headings, which are not rows, and inside the list's clip. Every other row, heading and count takes its new place at once, and the slot stays empty until the row lands; a heading stays whole and on top in every frame, so the row passes out of sight beneath it and comes out the other side. Staging the first row under the Unstaged heading once drew it over the whole heading, which had already taken the row's place, in the flight's first frame. Until the rows the write produces arrive, with a path filter set or not, the list shows its rows as they were, never empty and with no progress line opening above them, so nothing moves before the row does. Where the new slot lies outside the list's viewport the row does not travel, and a Stage or Unstage that fails, or leaves the row where it was, moves nothing. Stage all, Unstage all, staging the selected files, a refresh and changes made outside the app re-sort the list at once: many rows moving together explain nothing the eye can follow. The pointer that pressed the row's control stays where it was, and the new rows put another row's control, or a heading's Stage all, under it: whatever arrives there shows no hover fill and no tooltip until the pointer moves, with or without reduced motion, since the person never moved to it, and the pressed control takes its own tooltip with it, shown or still waiting out its delay. A click there still acts on it. Epsilon's Stage once lit up under the still pointer after gamma's Stage moved gamma away, its tooltip "Stage epsilon.txt" covering delta's row; then, with that fixed, gamma's own "Stage gamma.txt" appeared over epsilon's row a second after the press and stayed until the pointer moved.
- **The toolkit's entrances.** Dialogs and dropdown menus keep the toolkit's entrance: a dialog fades and slides in over 250 ms, a dropdown fades in as it drops 8 px over 150 ms.
- **Nothing else.** Selection, hover, focus rings, scrolling (no smooth or spring scrolling; a list that reveals a focused control lands in one frame), History's graph and the lists as they reflow, page and mode changes, loading (a local progress label, no shimmer) and copy feedback do not animate. Nor does the compact chrome switching at its breakpoint: the form is chosen before layout from the window's size so that no frame shows one form and then the other, and a transition there would either animate the workspace's height, laying History or Compare out again in every frame and moving the content being read, or fade the new form in, dimming its controls below their contrast rules and restarting at every crossing while the window is resized, and neither shows where a control went.
- **Reduced motion.** With the platform's reduced-motion preference (`App::reduce_motion`, which macOS supplies from its accessibility display settings and Linux will take from the desktop through the XDG settings portal), every transition is immediate: the first frame shows the final place, nothing departs and no frame is asked for, the toolkit's entrances finish in their first frame, and GIF playback and model clips pause (see Documents, media and exact source).

## Review checklist

Choose checks for the affected surface. History/comparison checks use merges, long paths, non-ASCII text, large commits, and relevant image/missing-content cases. Exercise commit → file → another file → Back, including a repository-wide search, a scrolled graph, file-history pages, and a rename boundary. Verify focus, Escape precedence, stale-preview rejection, pane resizing, chosen-column alignment, and clipping at regular/narrow widths in each affected theme. Check Split scrolling and Find from both sides and literal selection/copy across alignment blanks. Project, partial staging, commit, branch, recovery, and network tests use disposable repositories and local remotes; check exact destinations, stale plans, failures/conflicts, and unrelated staged/unstaged content. Settings checks include reopening, failed persistence, identity scope, and retained editor focus. External file/Git changes must refresh without disturbing the current interaction. Record the build and observed results separately; a screenshot is not performance evidence.
